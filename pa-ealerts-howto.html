How-to guide

How to set up E-Alerts for
your NAICS codes

Get notified automatically when the Commonwealth posts a solicitation matching what your business supplies.

E-Alerts is the Commonwealth's only push notification service for suppliers. The service matches solicitations to suppliers using NAICS codes, the industry classification codes you select for your business. This means the quality of your alerts is entirely determined by the quality of your codes, and getting them wrong produces no error message.

Most of this guide is about choosing codes well. The setup itself takes a few minutes.

Time required
20 to 30 minutes
Difficulty
Straightforward
Repeat
Review annually

📋 Before you start

  • An active Commonwealth vendor number. If you do not have one yet, complete steps 1 and 2 of the Quick Start Guide first
  • An email address you monitor daily. Alerts are time-sensitive and solicitations close
  • A clear picture of everything your business supplies, not only your main line of work

Steps

1
List everything you would take a contract for

Before opening any tool, write out every distinct product or service you would genuinely bid on. Include work that sits slightly outside your main line, work you have done before but do not advertise, and work you have the capacity to take on.

The most common cause of missed opportunities is a supplier who registers only the thing they are best known for. A commercial cleaning company that also supplies janitorial consumables and does light grounds maintenance has three lines of business, not one.

Result A written list of every service line, in plain language. Do not translate it into official terminology yet.
2
Find the NAICS code for each line

Look each one up at census.gov/naics using the keyword search. NAICS is a hierarchy: each code narrows from a broad sector down to a specific national industry, and the full code is always six digits.

Code Level Description
56 Sector Administrative, Support, Waste Management and Remediation
561 Subsector Administrative and Support Services
5617 Industry group Services to Buildings and Dwellings
56172 NAICS industry Janitorial Services
561720 National industry Janitorial Services

Record the full six-digit code for each line of business. A partial code is not usable for matching.

Result A six-digit code for every item on your list from step 1.
3
Check each code's scope before you commit to it

This is the step that determines whether the whole system works for you, and it is the one most suppliers skip. Read the full description for each code. Titles alone will mislead you.

  • Read the exclusions. Most NAICS entries explicitly list activities that belong to a different code. If your main work appears in an exclusion, you have the wrong code
  • Register both if two codes plausibly fit. There is no penalty for holding several, and buyers classify the same work differently across agencies
  • Check adjacent codes in the same industry group. Your neighbours in the hierarchy are often where your work actually gets posted

⚠️ Err toward more codes, not fewer

The cost of an irrelevant alert is the few seconds it takes to delete it. The cost of a missing code is a contract you never knew existed.

Result A final list of codes, each one checked against its full description rather than its title.
4
Confirm the same codes are on your supplier registration

Your NAICS codes appear in two places: on your Supplier Portal registration record, and in E-Alerts. Check the registration record first and update it if your final list differs from what is there.

The registration record is what agencies see when they look you up directly. E-Alerts is what reaches you automatically. A code in one but not the other may lead to missed opportunities.

Open PA Supplier Portal ↗
Result Your registration record lists every code from step 3.
5
Create your E-Alerts account

E-Alerts is a separate system from the Supplier Portal with its own login. Registering as a supplier does not create an E-Alerts account, and your Supplier Portal credentials will not work here.

Open E-Alerts ↗
Result You can sign in to E-Alerts and reach the subscription settings.
6
Add your codes and save

Enter each six-digit code from step 3 and save your subscription.

Result Every code from step 3 appears in your saved subscription list.

Verify it is working

Do not assume setup succeeded. This system fails quietly, and a subscription that sends nothing looks identical to a market with nothing in it.

✅ Three checks, in this order

  • Whitelist the sender immediately. Add the E-Alerts sending address to your safe senders list before you wait for anything. Commonwealth notifications are routinely filtered to spam, and that is the most common reason suppliers believe the service is not working
  • Wait one week, then check volume. If you have received nothing at all in seven days across several codes, something is wrong. Go to the troubleshooting table below
  • Cross-check by hand once. Search eMarketplace for a solicitation you would obviously want, then confirm you were alerted to it. The first two checks only prove that email is arriving. This one proves the matching works

Repeat the cross-check once a quarter for the first year. Your codes are a standing configuration, and configurations drift as your business changes.

Troubleshooting

Symptoms, most likely cause, and what to do about it.

Symptom Most likely cause Fix
No alerts at all Messages are being filtered, or the subscription was never saved Check spam and quarantine folders, whitelist the sender, then sign in and confirm your codes are listed
Alerts arrive, but not for opportunities you would obviously want Codes are too narrow, or the relevant line of business has no code registered Return to step 3. Check adjacent codes in the same industry group and add them
Large volume of irrelevant alerts One code is too broad and is matching an entire sector Identify the broadest code and replace it with two or three more specific ones rather than removing it outright
Alerts stopped arriving Email address changed, or the account lapsed Sign in and confirm the address on file, then re-save the subscription
You were alerted after the deadline had passed Alert timing does not guarantee adequate response time for every solicitation type Treat E-Alerts as a supplement to periodic manual checks on eMarketplace and JAGGAER, not a replacement
